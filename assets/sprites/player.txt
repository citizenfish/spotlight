; The player, seen from DIRECTLY ABOVE, walking. Three frames: a
; neutral stance and two strides.
;
; REDRAWN 2026-09-13 FROM ABOVE (issue #78), on the user's ruling after
; playing look-2 -- "the player and workers must be rendered as if
; overhead view ... it overrides all previous decisions" -- and then
; clarified the same day: "this is us looking top down onto the
; characters, as if we were directly above them. You should not see
; torso, only legs and arms moving." So there is NO BODY BELOW THE
; HEAD. Everything a person stacks vertically when seen from the front
; -- head, trunk, legs, feet -- is under the head when seen from above,
; and what shows is one compact mass of head and shoulders in the
; middle of the box, with the arms and the legs coming out from under
; it: the arms at the sides, the legs behind. The figure a row or two
; deep under the shoulders that the first plan-view drawings had was
; a body seen from the front and foreshortened, which is exactly what
; the user did not want. See the vault's "2026-09-13 People are drawn
; from above, and the rule goes".
;
; What he is: the LAMP at the front of his helmet (row 4, two pixels,
; centred, symmetric -- the helmet's top edge, up the screen, which is
; the way he walks); the HELMET, a disc six wide (rows 5-8); the
; SHOULDERS either side of it at the full width of the box (rows 6-7)
; -- the kit, his alone; his HANDS at the sides where the arms hang
; (rows 8-9, the edge columns); and his TOES together behind (rows
; 10-11). Rows 0-3 and 12-15 are clear in the neutral frame; the
; strides reach into them.
;
; THE WALK IS FOUR STEPS OVER THREE FRAMES: N A N B, advancing every
; FOUR PIXELS OF TRAVEL along either axis (walk.Stride, issue #72). A
; stride swings ONE ARM FORWARD -- a strip up the screen beside the
; helmet, cols 0-1, rows 2-4 -- and THE OTHER BACK -- a strip down
; beside the toes, cols 6-7, rows 8-10 -- and puts ONE LEG OUT BEHIND
; -- cols 2-3, rows 9-12 -- while the other is under him and out of
; sight. B is A in the mirror, so the figure stays centred over its
; column and never leans. The head and the shoulders (rows 5-7) are
; IDENTICAL in every frame: a walk is the limbs moving round a mass
; that does not, which is what an overhead walker looks like and what
; nothing seen from the front looks like.
;
; ONE ORIENTATION, HEAD TO THE TOP OF THE SCREEN, NO FACINGS. The light
; is what says which way you are looking. 2026-09-07's argument.
;
; THERE IS NO BAR UNDER HIS FEET (dropped at #72, kept dropped). The
; helmet disc six wide and the kit eight wide tell him from a
; follower's head disc four wide and shoulders six wide; the
; legibility contract passes without the bar, and -- pinned -- without
; the lamp too. He is a different body, not a different hat.
