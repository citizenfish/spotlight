; A worker you have freed, walking behind you, seen from DIRECTLY
; ABOVE. Three frames: a neutral stance and two strides.
;
; Why a third people sprite exists at all: raised arms MEAN "I still
; need reaching", and leaving them up on somebody already walking
; behind you is a lie the player would act on. A worker is drawn from
; their state, so a follower dropped back to waiting goes back to
; arms up on the frame they are dropped -- and keeps their stride,
; because the stride says which foot is forward and not what they are.
;
; REDRAWN 2026-09-13 FROM ABOVE (issue #78), with the player and for
; the same ruling -- see player.txt: no body below the head, one
; compact mass of head and shoulders with the limbs coming out from
; under it. The same person as the waiting worker: a HEAD that is a
; disc four wide (row 5), SHOULDERS six wide either side of it (rows
; 6-7), HANDS at the sides below the shoulders (rows 8-9), and TOES
; together behind (rows 10-11).
;
; What tells it from the player, where the eye is: no lamp -- row 4 is
; clear in the middle -- a head four wide under which he has a helmet
; six wide, and shoulders six wide where his are eight; and its hands
; and arms are a column inboard of his, because its shoulders are.
; The contract compares every frame of it against every frame of the
; player and the worker.
;
; The walk is the player's: N A N B, a step every four pixels the
; trail carries it, walk.Stride, stepped in Rescue.follow where the
; trail places the figure. One arm forward beside the head (cols 1-2,
; rows 3-4), the other back beside the toes (cols 5-6, rows 8-10), one
; leg out behind (cols 2-3, rows 9-12). Rows 5-7 never move. B is A
; in the mirror.
;

; derives the one-pixel halo mask (issue #70).
; Comments start with ';' rather than '#', which is what a row of eight
; set pixels starts with -- the first version of this format used '#'
; and silently ate the player's shoulders.
